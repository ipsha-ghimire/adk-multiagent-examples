# FREQUENCY OF LIGHT

## PLOT OUTLINE
**TITLE:** FREQUENCY OF LIGHT

**LOGLINE:**
Escaping a stifling marriage to an Austrian arms dealer on the eve of WWII, Hollywood’s most glamorous star, Hedy Lamarr, struggles to be recognized for her intellect. Teaming up with an eccentric avant-garde composer, she invents a revolutionary, un-jammable torpedo guidance system—only to have it dismissed by a military that prefers her beauty to her brains, laying the secret foundation for the future of wireless technology.

---

### ACT I: THE GILDED CAGE

*   **The Double Life:** In 1930s Austria, Hedwig Kiesler is a stunning young actress married to Fritz Mandl, a wealthy and controlling arms dealer. While Mandl treats her as a trophy, Hedwig uses her sharp, self-taught analytical mind to quietly absorb top-secret military discussions at his dinner parties—specifically, the vulnerability of radio-controlled torpedoes to enemy jamming.
*   **The Escape:** Horrified by her husband's growing ties to Nazi Germany, Hedwig orchestrates a daring, disguised escape from her castle in the dead of night, fleeing to London and eventually securing passage to America.
*   **A New Identity:** On the ship to America, she meets MGM mogul Louis B. Mayer. Recognizing her star potential, he renames her "Hedy Lamarr" and markets her as "the most beautiful woman in the world." 
*   **The Inciting Incident:** Hedy becomes an overnight Hollywood sensation, but she finds the studio system shallow and suffocating. To cope, she sets up an inventing table in her trailer, working on designs for improved traffic lights and aerodynamic wings for Howard Hughes. When WWII breaks out, she feels a burning duty to use her knowledge to help the Allied war effort.

---

### ACT II: THE FREQUENCY OF WAR

*   **An Unlikely Partnership:** At a Hollywood dinner party, Hedy meets George Antheil, an eccentric, avant-garde composer famous for his synchronized player-piano compositions. Hedy realizes that if they can synchronize a radio transmitter and receiver to "hop" frequencies in unison, the signal guiding a torpedo will be impossible for the Axis powers to jam.
*   **The Breakthrough:** Using the mechanics of George’s player pianos, they design a system that uses a punched paper roll to cycle through 88 different radio frequencies (matching the number of keys on a piano). 
*   **The Midpoint:** In 1942, Hedy and George successfully secure a patent for their "Secret Communication System." They proudly present it to the United States Navy, expecting to revolutionize naval warfare.
*   **The Rejection:** The Navy brass, blinded by sexism and Hollywood prejudice, dismisses the invention. They mockingly tell Hedy that if she wants to help the war effort, she should use her looks to sell war bonds, not try to design military hardware. The Navy classifies the patent as top-secret, locking it away and preventing Hedy from developing it further or seeking other buyers.
*   **The Downward Spiral:** Frustrated and heartbroken, Hedy returns to MGM. She throws herself into selling war bonds (raising millions) and acting, but the spark of her creative intellect is suppressed. As the years pass, her Hollywood career begins to wane, and she struggles with the tragedy of being adored for a face that is slowly aging, while her brilliant mind remains ignored.

---

### ACT III: THE LEGACY UNLOCKED

*   **The Silent Revival:** In 1962, during the height of the Cuban Missile Crisis, the U.S. Navy quietly unearths Hedy and George's expired patent to secure military communications during the blockade. Hedy, now living a quiet life away from the spotlight, discovers that her invention is finally being used to protect her country—though she receives no money or public credit.
*   **The Climax of Recognition:** Decades later, in the late 1990s, the digital revolution explodes. The principles of Hedy’s frequency-hopping technology are revealed to be the foundational bedrock for Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, and GPS. 
*   **Resolution:** An elderly, reclusive Hedy receives a phone call informing her that she is being honored with the Pioneer Award by the Electronic Frontier Foundation. Though she is too frail to accept in person, she records a poignant audio message. The film ends with a montage of modern society connected wirelessly, showing that the "most beautiful woman in the world" left behind a legacy of invisible, world-changing light.

**Classification:** Prestige Historical Drama / Biographical Tech-Thriller  

---

### Executive Summary
*Frequency of Light* possesses a highly marketable, high-concept biographical hook: **"The most beautiful woman in the world secretly invented the foundation for Wi-Fi."** This narrative seamlessly bridges the glamorous golden age of Hollywood with World War II espionage, scientific innovation, and a powerful feminist undertone. 

While mid-budget adult dramas face a challenging theatrical landscape post-2020, recent successes demonstrate that prestige historical biopics with strong thematic hooks and emotional resonance can still perform exceptionally well. With proper budgeting, an A-list lead actress, and a strategic award-season release window, *Frequency of Light* has the potential to be both a commercial success and an awards-circuit darling.

---

### Target Audience & Market Segments

1.  **The "Prestige" & Adult Drama Crowd (Ages 35+):** This demographic is highly loyal to theaters for well-reviewed, intellectual dramas. They drove the success of *Oppenheimer*, *The King's Speech*, and *One Life*.
2.  **Women in STEM / Feminist Cinema Enthusiasts:** The narrative of a brilliant woman fighting institutional sexism in the 1940s is incredibly timely. This group will champion the film on social media, driving organic word-of-mouth (similar to *Hidden Figures* and *Lessons in Chemistry*).
3.  **Classic Hollywood / Cinephile Demographic:** Film buffs will be drawn to the behind-the-scenes depiction of MGM, Louis B. Mayer, Howard Hughes, and the Golden Age studio system.
4.  **Tech-History Enthusiasts:** The unique mechanical integration of player pianos and radio technology appeals to audiences who love historical innovation and "how-it-was-made" stories.

---

### Commercial Strengths & Market Drivers

*   **The "Intellectual Property" of Truth:** The internet's fascination with Hedy Lamarr is already highly active. Viral articles, social media posts, and documentaries (like *Bombshell: The Hedy Lamarr Story*) have primed the global audience for a high-budget dramatization of her life.
*   **The Golden Age Hook:** Visually, the film offers double-value: the breathtaking glamour of 1930s/40s Hollywood fashion paired with the tense, gritty backdrop of WWII espionage.
*   **Dual-Acreage Appeal:** The film offers two distinct acts that can be marketed separately: Act I (thrilling escape from Austria/Hollywood rise) and Act II/III (the intellectual partnership and the tragic, bitter-sweet scientific legacy). This ensures a highly dynamic trailer.

---

### Risks & Mitigation Strategies

*   **Risk 1: The High Cost of Period Pieces.** Designing 1930s Austria, 1940s Hollywood, 1960s Cuba-era military rooms, and 1990s tech eras can quickly balloon a budget. 
    *   *Mitigation:* Keep the production budget strictly between **$30M – $45M**. Utilize tax-incentive locations (e.g., Eastern Europe for Austria, Georgia/Ohio for vintage studio lots) and rely on smart production design rather than CGI-heavy recreation.
*   **Risk 2: The Depressing Midpoint/Third Act.** The rejection of Hedy's patent by the Navy and her subsequent decline can leave audiences feeling deflated.
    *   *Mitigation:* The Third Act must heavily emphasize her ultimate vindication. Framing the 1990s digital revolution and the 1962 Cuban Missile Crisis use of her tech as a ticking-clock narrative victory will leave audiences feeling triumphant rather than depressed. Focus the ending on the *immortality of her mind* rather than the fading of her beauty.
*   **Risk 3: Casting Pressure.** The film lives or dies on finding an actress who can realistically project both the drop-dead glamour of "the most beautiful woman in the world" and the intense, sharp gravity of a brilliant mathematician.
    *   *Mitigation:* Cast a highly respected, bankable, and prestige-associated actress (e.g., Margot Robbie, Alicia Vikander, Lily James, or Ana de Armas) who can anchor the dramatic requirements while serving as a major box-office draw.

---

**Greenlight with Budget Controls.**  
*Frequency of Light* is a highly viable commercial venture. It turns a standard historical biopic into a compelling intellectual thriller. By keeping the budget modest ($35M range) and prioritizing a stellar lead actress, the film is perfectly positioned to capture both the prestige box office market and a massive, appreciative streaming audience worldwide.

## CASTING REPORT
Here are casting recommendations for the principal roles in **"FREQUENCY OF LIGHT,"** featuring actors who have received critical acclaim and fan enthusiasm for playing similar characters.

---

### 1. HEDY LAMARR (HEDWIG KIESLER)
*The brilliant Austrian-born star who struggles to be seen as more than "the most beautiful woman in the world." She is fiercely intelligent, fiercely independent, and harboring a deep-seated frustration with the superficiality of Hollywood.*

*   **Top Choice: ANA DE ARMAS**
    *   **Why she fits:** De Armas received an Academy Award nomination for her portrayal of Marilyn Monroe in *Blonde*, proving her extraordinary ability to capture the tragic disconnect between a global bombshell persona and a deeply complex, vulnerable inner life. Additionally, her roles in *Knives Out* and *Blade Runner 2049* showcase her sharp, commanding screen presence. She has the exact mix of old-school glamour and expressive, intelligent depth required to capture Hedy's dual existence.
*   **Alternative Choice: ALICIA VIKANDER**
    *   **Why she fits:** Vikander won an Oscar for *The Danish Girl* and captivated audiences as a brilliant, calculating mind in *Ex Machina*. She is highly skilled in period dramas, possesses an elegant, classic beauty, and is exceptional at playing fiercely intelligent women operating in restrictive, male-dominated environments.

---

### 2. GEORGE ANTHEIL
*An eccentric, high-energy, avant-garde composer. He is manic, passionate, and sees the world through the lens of rhythm and mechanics, making him the perfect creative partner to help Hedy realize her frequency-hopping invention.*

*   **Top Choice: PAUL DANO**
    *   **Why he fits:** Dano is widely celebrated for playing brilliant but deeply eccentric and obsessive creatives. Crucially, his Oscar-adjacent performance as the genius, troubled composer Brian Wilson in *Love & Mercy* is a direct parallel to George Antheil. Dano has a unique ability to portray frantic, passionate intellectual energy that would bounce beautifully off of Hedy’s analytical focus.
*   **Alternative Choice: ANDREW GARFIELD**
    *   **Why he fits:** Garfield earned immense critical acclaim and an Oscar nomination for *Tick, Tick... Boom!*, where he played a manic, brilliant composer racing against time. Garfield excels at portraying infectious enthusiasm, rapid-fire intellect, and artistic obsession, making him a fantastic fit for Antheil's eccentric genius.

---

### 3. FRITZ MANDL
*Hedy’s first husband. A wealthy, charismatic, but deeply controlling and dangerous Austrian arms dealer with ties to the rising Nazi regime. He treats Hedy as a trophy but inadvertently teaches her about military technology.*

*   **Top Choice: MADS MIKKELSEN**
    *   **Why he fits:** Mikkelsen is the gold standard for sophisticated, menacing, and highly intelligent antagonists (*Casino Royale*, *Hannibal*, *Indiana Jones and the Dial of Destiny*). He can effortlessly balance the charming, wealthy high-society host with the chilling, possessive, and dangerous arms dealer who forces Hedy to escape in the dead of night.
*   **Alternative Choice: CLAES BANG**
    *   **Why he fits:** The Danish actor received widespread praise for his charismatic and deeply menacing performance in BBC’s *Dracula* and his role as a wealthy, arrogant art dealer in *The Burnt Orange Heresy*. He has the towering, imposing physical presence and European sophistication needed to play Mandl.

---

### 4. LOUIS B. MAYER
*The legendary, tyrannical, and paternalistic head of MGM. He is a shrewd businessman who renames Hedy and treats her like a prized studio asset, completely blind to her intellectual capabilities.*

*   **Top Choice: PAUL GIAMATTI**
    *   **Why he fits:** Giamatti is a master at playing blustery, powerful, and deeply cynical authority figures who are simultaneously human and frustratingly set in their ways (recently acclaimed in *The Holdovers*, *Billions*, and *Cinderella Man*). He would bring the perfect blend of theatrical showmanship and bottom-line-driven ruthlessness to the MGM mogul.
*   **Alternative Choice: GARY OLDMAN**
    *   **Why he fits:** Oldman is a chameleon who received immense praise for his work in *Mank*, which is set in the exact same era of Golden Age Hollywood. Having already navigated the studio-system politics of the 1930s and 40s on screen, Oldman would bring an incredibly authentic, commanding, and historically resonant energy to the role of Mayer.
